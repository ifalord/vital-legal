Medical Disclaimer

Last updated: November 11, 2026

⚠ Vital does not diagnose or treat, and does not replace a professional

Vital helps you organize your medications and health information. It is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, mitigate or prevent any disease, and it has not been reviewed, cleared or approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) or any other regulator. Several features use artificial intelligence (AI): their answers are generated automatically, can be wrong or incomplete, and do not come from a doctor, nurse or pharmacist. Before making any decision about your health or your medications, talk to your doctor or pharmacist. If you think you may have a medical emergency, call 911 (in the U.S.) or your local emergency number now. If you are thinking about suicide or self-harm, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, U.S.).

1. What Vital does

Vital is a self-management app that helps you:

  • Remember when to take your medications.
  • Keep a journal of how you feel, what you eat, and what symptoms you have.
  • Record vital measurements you capture yourself (blood pressure, glucose, weight, etc.).
  • Talk with an assistant that organizes the information you provide.
  • Share selected information with the caregivers you designate.
  • Prepare summaries you can take to your medical appointment.

2. What Vital does NOT do

  • It does not diagnose disease. Patterns detected by AI are observations about your data, not diagnoses, and they do not show that a medication causes or relieves anything.
  • It does not prescribe medication or adjust dosages.
  • It does not provide medical advice. Its suggestions are informational and general in nature.
  • It is not an emergency service. If you have a medical emergency, call 911 in the U.S. or your local emergency number.
  • It is not a crisis service. If you are thinking about suicide or self-harm, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, U.S.) or chat at 988lifeline.org; outside the U.S., contact your local crisis line. If you are in danger now, call 911 or your local emergency number.
  • It does not replace professional management of a chronic illness.

3. Artificial Intelligence: important limits

Vital uses AI models for several features. These models:

  • Can make mistakes. They may return information that is incorrect, out of date, or incomplete.
  • Do not know your full medical history — only what you enter into the app.
  • Have no clinical judgment. They operate on statistical patterns, not medical reasoning.
  • Can hallucinate. They may invent details, especially around specific drugs, doses, or effects.

The assistant is an AI, not a person or a health professional. It cannot examine you and cannot assess photos of your skin, wounds or injuries: Vital tells it not to say what is in such a photo, whether it is serious or whether it is infected, and to suggest that a professional see it. Anything it says about a photo (of your skin, a wound, a medication or a meal) may be wrong, and if it does not mention a warning sign, that does not mean there is none. Do not use the chat in an emergency: call 911 or your local emergency number.

When a message you send the assistant, typed or dictated, seems to describe a medical emergency or thoughts of harming yourself, the chat shows a card with the numbers to call (911, and 988 for thoughts of self-harm). The card looks for certain words and phrases in English and Spanish: it can miss a message that says it in other words, and it can appear when there is no emergency. If you think you may have an emergency, do not wait for the card or for the assistant's reply: call 911 or your local emergency number.

Before acting on anything the AI tells you, verify the information with your healthcare provider.

4. Pill identification by photo

The camera-based pill identification feature gives an AI's guess and is informational only. Many pills look visually identical but contain different active ingredients. Never take a medication based solely on Vital's visual identification. Always verify with the original packaging, the patient information leaflet, or your pharmacist.

5. Prescription reading by photo

The prescription reading feature uses AI to read the text in your photo and can misread names, doses or times. Always review each field against the original prescription before saving.

6. Drug interaction checker

Interaction checks are informational and not exhaustive. They are generated by a general-purpose AI model (today Google Gemini) from the names of the medications on your list; Vital does not look them up in a validated drug-interaction database, and no pharmacist has validated them. They can miss interactions or rate them wrongly, and they do not consider doses, food, alcohol, herbal products or supplements you have not added, allergies, pregnancy, your age or your health conditions. A result with no interactions does not mean that a combination is safe. Do not start, stop or change any medication because of it; ask your doctor or pharmacist. If your list has more than 12 medications that came from a caregiver with names the check does not recognise as medications, the check cannot be done and no interaction is checked: ask your pharmacist to review your list, or change those names to the medication's name as it appears on the package.

7. Medication reminders

Reminders are notifications scheduled on your phone. They reach you only if:

  • Vital may send notifications, and the phone's settings (Silent mode, Focus, Do Not Disturb) let them through; otherwise they may not sound or may not show.
  • The device has battery and is powered on.
  • The app has not been deleted, and you are still signed in on that phone (signing out removes its reminders).
  • After a change made on another phone, you have opened Vital on your phone: that is when it sets up the new reminders.

Vital does not guarantee reminder delivery. If a dose is critical (anticoagulants, antiepileptics, insulin, etc.), do not rely on the app alone — use a redundant system (an additional alarm, a physical pill organizer, a reminder from a family member).

8. Caregivers

You choose what each caregiver sees. Alerts to caregivers are created by our servers when a dose is marked as skipped, and about 2 hours after the time of a dose that has not been marked (the latter only once the patient's phone has opened Vital and shared its time zone); after 2 days without any dose recorded, caregivers get one notice instead of one per dose. An alert depends on our servers, Apple's and Expo's notification services and the caregiver's phone, and on the caregiver being able to act. A dose marked as taken is what the patient marked, not proof that it was taken. Vital does not guarantee that an alert is sent, arrives or is answered. In an emergency, call 911 (or your local emergency number) directly.

9. Wearable data and sample figures

Vital does not currently connect to wearables, Apple Health or Health Connect, and it does not read data from any of them. The sleep, steps and heart-rate figures shown in the Health tab are sample data for illustration, and the app marks them as such. They are not your measurements, so do not use them to make any health decision.

10. When NOT to use Vital

  • In a medical emergency (sudden chest pain, difficulty breathing, loss of consciousness, severe bleeding, signs of stroke). Call 911 or your local emergency number.
  • To self-medicate based on its suggestions.
  • To stop a prescribed treatment without consulting your doctor.
  • As the sole reference for administering medication to a minor or dependent — always with professional supervision.

11. Good uses of Vital

  • Organizing your medications and dosing schedules.
  • Keeping a journal you can later share with your doctor.
  • Capturing questions you want to ask at your next appointment.
  • Spotting patterns worth discussing with your healthcare provider.
  • Sharing your progress with your support network.
