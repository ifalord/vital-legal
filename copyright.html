2. What your notice must include

Under 17 U.S.C. §512(c)(3), your notice must include:

  • your physical or electronic signature;
  • the copyrighted work you claim is infringed;
  • the material you claim is infringing, and enough information for us to find it (for example, the email address of the account and where the material appears in the app);
  • your address, telephone number and email address;
  • a statement that you believe in good faith that the use is not authorized by the copyright owner, its agent or the law; and
  • a statement that the information in your notice is accurate and, under penalty of perjury, that you are authorized to act on behalf of the owner.

3. What we do with a valid notice

When we receive a valid notice, we remove or disable access to the material and tell the user who stored it.

4. Counter-notice

If your material was removed and you believe it was a mistake, you may send our agent a counter-notice with:

  • your physical or electronic signature;
  • the material that was removed and where it appeared before it was removed;
  • a statement, under penalty of perjury, that you believe in good faith that the material was removed as a result of a mistake or misidentification; and
  • your name, address and telephone number, and a statement that you consent to the jurisdiction of the federal district court for the judicial district where your address is (or, if your address is outside the United States, any judicial district in which IFA- TECH LLC may be found) and that you will accept service of process from the person who sent the notice or their agent.

When we receive a valid counter-notice, we send a copy to the person who sent the notice and restore the material no less than 10 and no more than 14 business days after receiving the counter-notice, unless our agent first learns from that person that they have filed a court action to stop the infringement.

5. Repeat infringers

In appropriate circumstances, we close the accounts of users who repeatedly infringe copyright.

6. Misrepresentations

Anyone who knowingly misrepresents that material infringes, or that it was removed by mistake, may be liable for damages (17 U.S.C. §512(f)).
