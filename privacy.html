1. What data we collect

1.1 Data you provide directly

  • Account: email, password (we keep only its bcrypt hash, never the password itself), preferred language. If you sign in with Apple instead, Apple gives Vital an identifier for your account, your email address or, if you choose to hide it, a private relay address that Apple creates for Vital, and, the first time only, your name. Vital never receives your Apple ID password, and such an account has no Vital password.
  • Profile: name, phone number (optional), year of birth, sex, weight, height, blood type, allergies, chronic conditions, emergency contact. Section 3.1 says what your caregivers can see of it.
  • Medications: name, dose, schedule, instructions and stock and, if you open a medication's information, the text the AI wrote about it. A photo you take or choose to identify a pill or read a prescription is sent to the AI features you allow (Section 11) and is not stored on our servers.
  • Health journal: mood, meals, symptoms, vital measurements (blood pressure, glucose, weight, temperature, heart rate, oxygen saturation) and what you note on the body: pain, skin spots, burns, bruises, wounds, fractures, swelling or itching, with the exact spot you mark on the figure, your answers and your notes.
  • Conversations with the assistant: what you type or dictate and the photos you send. Vital does not store the conversation: it stays in the app while the app is open. What the assistant offers to record for you (a medication, a symptom…) is saved, like any other entry, only when you tap “Save” on its card; if your app has not yet been updated to show that button, its offers wait on our server for 30 minutes (Section 11.2).
  • Medical appointments: doctor or center, specialty, date and time, place, notes.
  • Caregivers: the emails of people you invite, the relationship you note, the permissions you grant them, the medications they propose for your list (with who proposed them and whether you added them), the alerts Vital sends them about your doses and the comments written on those alerts.

1.2 Data we collect automatically

  • Push notification token (Apple/Google), registered to your account on this phone with the language of the notification texts, the app version, the platform (iOS or Android) and a random identifier of this installation, not the device's hardware ID. If another account signs in on the phone, the token moves to that account.
  • Time zone and language of your phone: the time zone so that our server knows your local day and time (to record a dose not marked 2 hours after its time, and to give your caregivers the times on your clock), and the language so that the notifications and alerts our server writes for you come in it.
  • Connection data that our providers record with each request: your IP address, the type of device and app (user agent) and the time. Supabase keeps it with each session while the session lasts and in its logs for up to 7 days. Each time the app starts, it also asks Expo whether there is an update, which gives Expo this data, the app version and a random identifier of this installation (Section 4).
  • Use of AI features: how many times each one called the AI model for you in the current 24-hour period, to apply the daily limit.

1.3 Data we do NOT collect

  • GPS location.
  • Contact list.
  • Browsing history.
  • Financial information (the application does not currently collect any payment).

1.4 Data stored on your phone

So that it works without waiting for the network and reminds you on time, Vital keeps some data in its own storage on your phone, which other apps cannot read:

  • Your session, so you do not have to sign in every time.
  • Your reminders: for each medication, its name, dose and time; for each upcoming medical appointment, the doctor and the time. They are scheduled with your phone's notification system.
  • The latest results of the AI features you allow: daily suggestions and detected patterns, so they appear as soon as you open the app.
  • Interaction ratings: for each pair of your medications, the most serious rating an interaction check showed you and its explanation, so that a later check never shows that pair as milder; and, if you mark a pair as reviewed with your pharmacist, the date and the rating at that time. Also the names of the medications on your list that this phone treats as checked (those in the last check you saw finish or, the first time, those you already had), so that any added without their check finishing get checked.
  • Your AI permission decision (allowed or turned off, with its date and version), so the app knows at once whether it may use the AI features.
  • The body-map figure you picked, if you signed up as a caregiver (or as both patient and caregiver).
  • A 24-hour pause on creating an account, only if a year of birth under 14 was entered on this phone (Section 9): the moment it ends, and nothing else. It ends by itself.
  • Temporary copies of your data export, of the medical report you share, and of the photos and voice recordings you send to the AI features (a recording is deleted once it has been sent).
  • Your preferences on this phone: language, theme and voice, and the random identifiers of this installation used for notifications and app updates (Section 1.2).

Apart from what each feature sends when you use it (Sections 4 and 11), this data stays on your phone. Vital asks your phone to leave it out of backups and out of transfers to a new phone (the reminders themselves are kept by the phone's notification system). Signing out removes the reminders (they are set up again when you sign in), the AI results and the session from this phone; deleting your account removes everything listed above except your preferences; uninstalling the app removes all of it. Section 6 gives each period.

3.1 Your caregivers

A caregiver is someone you invite. When you invite someone, you choose the sections they can see: the invitation goes out with every section allowed, so uncheck what you do not want to share before sending it. Only the Vital account with the email address you invited can accept, and it becomes your caregiver only when you approve it (the app shows you that account's email address); until then, it sees none of your sections. The invitation link works for 7 days; sharing it again makes a new link, which works for 7 days, and the old one stops working. Send the link only to the person you are inviting: anyone who has it can decline the invitation. The email address you invited is erased from the invitation after 30 days (Section 6).

You can change what each caregiver sees at any time in Caregiver → Your caregivers → Permissions, or take their access away altogether (“Remove access”). A caregiver can also stop being your caregiver at any time; we send you a notification when they do.

  • What they can see, if you allow it: your medications and each day's doses (taken, late, not taken or pending), the dose history and your adherence; your measurements; your meals; your mood; your symptoms, pain and what you note on the body; your medical appointments (upcoming ones and those of the last 24 hours); your health profile (year of birth, weight, height, blood type, allergies, chronic conditions and emergency contact); and your medical report with those same sections (without the AI summary). With “Symptoms, pain and body entries” or “Health profile” allowed, they can also read your sex, which the app uses to draw the body figure.
  • What every caregiver you approve sees: your name and, if you added it, your phone number, so they can call you or write to you on WhatsApp.
  • What they can do, if you allow it: propose medications for your list (they cannot change or delete anything). We tell you with a notification (“Ana suggested a medication for your list”), and it waits in the Medications tab until you add it or turn it down: until you add it, it does not remind you, does not count in your doses, is not checked for interactions and is not sent to the AI features. Once you add it, its reminders start ringing on your phone and its doses count from then on.
  • What they are told: a notification for each dose you take (they can turn these off) and for each dose you do not take: skipped, or not marked 2 hours after its time. After 2 days with no dose recorded, one notice instead of one per dose. The notification says who and what happened (“María didn't take a dose”), never the medication or the dose; the details are in the app. Notifications can arrive late or not at all (see the Medical Disclaimer).
  • If you are a caregiver: a patient whose invitation you accept sees your name and the email address of your account, never the rest of your profile, and the medications you propose show your name. The email address a patient invited you with is erased from the invitation after 30 days (Section 6); we use it to send you the invitation, and your account to send you their alerts. You can stop being a patient's caregiver at any time (“Stop caring for …”); we let them know.

If part of your data cannot be read, the app shows an error instead of creating an incomplete file.   • Correct inaccurate data: edit your profile and your entries in the app, or ask us by email.
  • Delete your data (right to erasure): Settings → Privacy and data → “Delete my account”, or ask us by email. Deleting your account also erases what Vital keeps on the phone where you do it, except your preferences (Section 1.4); on another phone where you used Vital, uninstall the app to erase its copy. If you signed in with Apple, you can also end Vital's access to your Apple ID at any time in your Apple Account settings (Sign in with Apple).
  • Restrict processing.
  • Object to processing based on legitimate interest.
  • Port your data to another provider: the JSON file is structured and machine-readable.
  • Withdraw consent at any time, without affecting the lawfulness of prior processing: your AI permission in Settings → Privacy and data, a caregiver's access in Caregiver → Your caregivers, and your health-data consent by deleting your account.
  • Not be subject to automated decisions with legal or similarly significant effects. AI suggestions in Vital are advisory and reviewable by you — never automatic.

To exercise any right, write to vital-app@ifa-tech.com from the email address of your Vital account. 8. Security

We apply appropriate technical and organizational measures:

  • Encryption in transit: the app and our servers communicate only over TLS 1.2 or 1.3.
  • Encryption at rest: Supabase encrypts the database with AES-256.
  • Password hashing: we keep only a bcrypt hash of your password, never the password itself.
  • Per-user isolation (RLS): Row Level Security policies limit each account to its own data and to what a patient shares with the caregivers they invite and approve (Section 3.1 says what a patient and a caregiver can read of each other). Our server functions and scheduled database jobs use elevated access only for the tasks this Policy describes, such as deleting your account, sending notifications, recording doses not marked, and the interaction check and its shared answers.
  • Restricted access: access to the production database is limited to the company's administrator, directly or through the tools the administrator uses to run the service, and only to operate and support it; our providers' staff can access it only as their terms allow.
  • Auditing: we keep a record of sensitive actions in your account (Section 6), and our database provider keeps technical request logs for up to 7 days.
  • Backups: daily and encrypted, kept for 7 days by our database provider.
  • Breach notification: if a breach of security affects your information, we will tell you without unreasonable delay, by email and in the app, within the deadlines the law sets (for example, 30 days under Florida law and 60 days under the FTC Health Breach Notification Rule), and we will also notify the FTC, state authorities and the media when those laws require it.

11. Artificial intelligence

Vital's AI features reach an AI model through OpenRouter. Today all of them use Google's Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite model, which Google runs on Google Cloud Vertex AI. The data you send them (text, voice, photos and the health entries listed in Section 11.2) is processed in real time to produce the answer. The AI services use it only to produce the answers, readings, checks, suggestions and summaries you see in Vital, never for advertising or to train their models, and Vital does not use your data to train AI models. Every request also asks OpenRouter to use only model providers that, by OpenRouter's classification, do not collect user data, rather than providers that may store it or train on it.

11.1 Your permission

AI features send part of your information to AI services run by other companies. Until you allow them, nothing is sent and the AI features stay off.

  • It is your explicit consent, separate from this Policy. You give it inside the app, in its own prompt, before the first AI feature runs. One permission covers all AI features. Accepting this Policy does not give it.
  • It is recorded. We save each decision (allowed or turned off) with its date and the version of the explanation shown to you.
  • You can withdraw it at any time in Settings → Privacy and data, without deleting your account. From then on nothing more is sent; a withdrawal made without a connection reaches our server when the phone is back online. Withdrawing cannot recall information that has already been processed.

11.2 What each AI feature sends

Once you have allowed AI features, these are the features that send information to OpenRouter, and what each one sends. Some run by themselves; the rest run when you use them.

  • Assistant chat. What you type or say to the assistant, the photo you send it if you add one (for example of a medication, your skin, a wound or a meal), and the recent messages of the conversation (up to the last 30, and at most 16,000 characters), including the assistant's offers to save something and what you did with each one. A photo goes with its own message only, without the details stored in its file about where, when and with which device it was taken (our server removes them before sending it on), and Vital does not store it; earlier messages mention it only with the word “[foto]” (Spanish for “photo”, as the app sends it in either language). It runs when you send a message. What the assistant offers to save is saved only when you tap “Save”, which sends nothing to the AI model: our server checks it again and writes it to your account. If your app has not yet been updated to show “Save”, the reply lists the offers with a 4-digit code, you save them by replying “save” and the code (for example “save 4821”), and our server keeps them for 30 minutes and then deletes them (Section 6).
  • Voice dictation. The recording of your voice (at most 10 minutes), to turn it into text. It runs when you hold to talk to the assistant.
  • Pill and prescription photos. The photo you take or choose, without the details stored in its file about where, when and with which device it was taken (our server removes them before sending it on). A prescription photo can show your name and your doctor's. Vital does not store it. It runs when you take or choose a photo to identify a pill or read a prescription.
  • Medication information and interaction checks. The name of the medication you look up or, for an interaction check, the names of the active medications on your list, which our server reads from your account. Doses and other details are not sent. They run when you open a medication's information or the interaction check; an interaction check also runs by itself when you save or rename a medication, when you save one the assistant offered, when you add one a caregiver proposed, and when you open the Medications tab with a medication whose check did not finish, to compare it with your others.
  • Daily suggestions. A snapshot of your day: how many of today's doses were taken, skipped, missed or are still pending; today's mood; whether you logged pain today; your adherence over the last 7 days; and the symptoms and pain you logged in the last 3 days. It runs by itself when you open Vital.
  • Pattern detection. Your saved medications (name, dose and start date) and, for the last 30 days, the status of each scheduled dose, your symptoms, pain entries, mood, stress and energy check-ins, and manual measurements such as blood pressure or glucose. It runs by itself in the background after you save a medication or a measurement, or a medication, symptom, pain entry or measurement the assistant offered, and whenever you ask for it from the Health tab.
  • Report summary. From the medical report for the last 30 days: adherence figures, your medications (name, dose and times of day), symptoms and pain entries with their dates, and your average mood. Before the data is sent on, our server keeps only these fields, so your name, email and account ID are left out. It runs by itself when you open the medical report.

AI responses may contain errors and never constitute medical advice. Read the full Medical Disclaimer.
